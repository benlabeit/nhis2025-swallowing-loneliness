SUPPLEMENTARY STATISTICAL RESULTS
NHIS 2025: self-reported swallowing problems and loneliness in adults aged 65+
6 November 2026

Status and scope
The supplementary plan was documented after the primary findings were known and
before the additional analyses were run. These analyses are supportive, not new
confirmatory primary analyses. The original exposure definition, primary outcome,
primary M3 model and primary results have not been changed. All analyses retain
the NHIS final adult weight, public-use strata and PSUs, with domain analyses
formed after defining the design on the full adult file. Income-adjusted models
use all ten official income imputations.

1. Model-standardized absolute prevalence
Survey-logistic models were standardized over the survey-weighted covariate
distribution of the analytic sample using survey::svypredmeans. For M3, the
standardized prevalence of frequent loneliness was 4.27% (95% CI 3.77-4.83)
without swallowing problems and 7.94% (6.27-10.01) with swallowing problems.
The absolute prevalence difference was 3.67 percentage points (1.75-5.60), and
the marginal prevalence ratio was 1.861 (1.430-2.421), in 8,094 participants.
These are standardized associations, not causal effects. The marginal ratio is
not the identical estimand as the primary conditional Poisson PR of 1.835.

In the identical depression-complete sample (N=8,055), the model without
depressive-feelings adjustment yielded standardized prevalences of 4.27% and
7.96%, difference 3.70 percentage points (1.76-5.63), and marginal PR 1.866
(1.433-2.430). Additional adjustment for depressive feelings yielded 4.38%
(3.86-4.97) and 6.64% (5.24-8.38), difference 2.26 percentage points
(0.60-3.92), and marginal PR 1.516 (1.161-1.980). The reduction does not
establish mediation or resolve whether depressive feelings precede the exposure.

For the broader endpoint (always/usually/sometimes lonely; N=8,094), standardized
prevalences were 22.92% (21.82-24.05) and 29.26% (25.75-33.04), difference
6.34 percentage points (2.58-10.11), and marginal PR 1.277 (1.119-1.456).
Logistic models keep individual fitted probabilities between zero and one,
addressing the prediction-range problem seen in the earlier Poisson sensitivity
models. This does not prove that the logistic mean specification is correct.

Variance and pooling
Within each imputation, the predictive-margin covariance includes the model
coefficient and estimated covariate-distribution components supplied by survey.
Prevalences were pooled on the logit scale, differences on the identity scale,
and prevalence ratios on the log scale after delta-method transformation.
Rubin pooling used finite complete-data residual design degrees of freedom.
Consequently a pooled contrast need not equal an arithmetic operation on the
separately transformed and rounded pooled prevalences. Direct weighted
standardization was checked against svypredmeans within 1e-7 in every fit.

2. Missingness and response-weighting sensitivity
The common complete sample includes 8,094 of 8,492 confirmed older adults.
Excluded participants had a larger weighted share aged 85+ among those with
known age (15.50% versus 9.41%) and a larger weighted share of proxy interviews
(8.62% versus 4.59%). These comparisons are descriptive and do not establish
missing at random. The 24 persons classified as >=65 only through AGE65 remain
outside the age-adjusted response-weighting target.

The response model targeted the 8,468 adults with numerical age >=65 and used
age group, region, adult household count, any children in the household and
proxy status, all fully observed. Fitted inclusion probabilities ranged from
0.7747 to 0.9721. The weighted inclusion fraction was 0.9530. Stabilized
response multipliers among included cases ranged from 0.9804 to 1.2301; the
median was 0.9943 and the 99th percentile 1.0563. The combined-weight Kish
effective sample size was 6,024; this describes unequal weights and is not the
full design-based effective sample size accounting for clustering.

The response-weighted M3 Poisson PR was 1.830 (95% CI 1.411-2.373). The joint
estimating-equation linearization includes uncertainty from estimating the
response-propensity coefficients. The analytic derivative of the outcome score
with respect to those coefficients was used to correct the outcome influence
function; score contributions were aggregated within the original design's
strata and PSUs. The outcome-only covariance component reproduced the standard
survey::svyglm covariance with a maximum absolute discrepancy of 5.3e-12 over
the ten imputations. The propensity-only covariance was also checked.

Winsorizing response multipliers at their unweighted 1st and 99th percentiles
affected 74 upper-tail records and no lower-tail records. The resulting PR was
1.832 (1.413-2.376). Its confidence interval treats the estimated/truncated
weights as fixed, so this is a conditional diagnostic rather than a second
fully propagated uncertainty analysis.

The similarity to the primary estimate supports robustness to selection
associated with the limited observed baseline features in the response model.
It does not exclude bias from unmeasured predictors of missingness, incorrect
response-model specification, or missingness depending directly on unobserved
loneliness or swallowing problems. Extrapolation to institution residents
remains unsupported.

3. Table 1 and NCHS precision assessment
Table 1 files provide weighted categorical characteristics, unweighted category
counts, valid denominators and explicit missingness for both the available-data
exposure groups and the common complete-case sample. The income table pools
weighted category proportions across ten imputations and reports the range of
unweighted category counts. Imputed income is not presented as observed income.
No baseline significance tests were added.

All six primary descriptive prevalence estimates passed the implemented NCHS
proportion criteria, as did their complements. All 164 non-income Table 1 rows
and all ten five-category loneliness proportions also passed. The new precision
tables use Korn-Graubard beta limits with effective sample sizes capped at nominal
sample size and a finite-df adjustment. The original primary figures used logit
limits and remain unchanged; these two interval constructions are not identical.
Income rows report pooled percentages and standard errors, with n-ranges; the
non-imputed NCHS proportion screen was not mechanically applied to the pooled
income percentages. No NCHS proportion rule was applied to regression PRs.

For participants with swallowing problems, frequent loneliness was 10.34%
(Korn-Graubard 95% CI 8.04-13.04), versus 4.17% (3.67-4.71) without problems.
The corresponding effective denominators were about 610 and 5,886, compared
with nominal denominators of 686 and 7,545. The design degrees of freedom
for these domains were 334 and 561, respectively. These checks address
statistical precision, not measurement validity or selection bias.

4. Full loneliness distribution and ordinal-model diagnostic
Weighted category percentages (no swallowing problems / swallowing problems)
were: never 54.76/39.45; rarely 22.83/25.06; sometimes 18.23/25.15;
usually 1.80/4.49; always 2.37/5.86. Denominators were 7,545 and 686,
respectively. Categories sum to 100% within each group up to rounding.

Four cumulative survey-logistic models with M3 adjustment yielded exposure ORs:
- At least rarely versus never: 1.495 (95% CI 1.217-1.836).
- At least sometimes versus rarely/never: 1.453 (1.178-1.792).
- At least usually versus sometimes/rarely/never: 1.998 (1.479-2.699).
- Always versus all other responses: 1.923 (1.301-2.842).
These are odds ratios, not prevalence ratios, and are supplemental threshold-
specific summaries rather than additional primary tests.

Cross-threshold coefficient covariance was estimated jointly using survey
influence functions. Sixty-nine contrasts compared 23 non-intercept slopes
across three alternative thresholds with the primary >=usually threshold.
Rubin pooling preceded a conservative Bonferroni diagnostic. One contrast
rejected slope equality: family poverty ratio >=400% versus <100%, at least
rarely compared with at least usually (unadjusted p=0.000543; adjusted p=0.0375).
The exposure's own three slope contrasts did not reject after this correction.
Because the full proportional-odds specification was not supported, no single
common ordinal OR was fitted or promoted. Failure of a nuisance slope is enough
to question the specified common-slope model; it is not evidence that the
swallowing association necessarily differs across thresholds. A partial
proportional-odds model would require further explicitly exploratory work.

Key limitations for the manuscript
All supplementary decisions were made after seeing the primary findings.
Model standardization remains associational; the exposure and several
covariates may have different or overlapping time windows. Response weighting
requires assumptions that the data cannot establish. The ordinal diagnostic
is conservative and still only a model-assumption check. Supplementary effect
estimates were not multiplicity-adjusted, except the explicitly labeled
proportional-odds diagnostic. Primary and supportive findings should be
distinguished in the manuscript, and no causal mediation effect should be claimed.
