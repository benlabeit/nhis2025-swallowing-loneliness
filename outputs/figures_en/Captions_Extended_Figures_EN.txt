Supplementary Figure S2. Model-standardized prevalence of frequent loneliness
by swallowing problems among adults aged 65 years or older, NHIS 2025.
Points and horizontal lines show standardized prevalences and 95% confidence
intervals from survey-weighted logistic models with the M3 covariates. Frequent
loneliness was defined as always or usually feeling lonely. Swallowing problems
refer to self-reported difficulties during the preceding 12 months. Both exposure
levels were standardized over the survey-weighted covariate distribution of the
same complete-case sample of 8,094 adults, including 429 outcome events. Covariates
were age group, sex, education, family income-to-poverty ratio, partnership, living
alone, mobility, hearing, stroke history, in-person contact and telephone/video
contact. Estimates and variances were pooled over ten official income imputations.
Prevalences were pooled on the logit scale, the absolute difference on its natural
scale and the marginal prevalence ratio on the log scale, so the pooled contrasts
need not exactly equal arithmetic operations on the separately rounded pooled
prevalences. These model-standardized associations are not causal effects. The
marginal PR is not the same parameter as the primary conditional Poisson PR.

Supplementary Figure S3. Survey-weighted distribution of all five loneliness
response categories by swallowing problems among adults aged 65 years or older,
NHIS 2025.
Points represent weighted category percentages and horizontal lines show
Korn-Graubard 95% confidence intervals. Blue circles indicate participants without
swallowing problems; teal diamonds indicate participants with swallowing problems.
The analysis includes 7,545 and 686 participants, respectively, with valid exposure
and outcome responses; the two groups contain 8,231 adults in total. Percentages
sum to 100% within each exposure group before rounding. The displayed sample sizes
are unweighted. These distributions are unadjusted for covariates; all ten category
proportions satisfy the implemented NCHS precision criteria. The primary binary
outcome combines the usually and always categories. No common proportional-odds
estimate is superimposed because the planned diagnostic did not support the full
common-slope specification. The distributions and uncertainty intervals describe
cross-sectional self-report data and do not establish direction or causation.
