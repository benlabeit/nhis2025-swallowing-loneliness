NHIS 2025: Swallowing problems and loneliness among adults aged 65 years or older
Figure captions and usage notes
English edition, 6 November 2026. Estimates are unchanged from the original analysis.

Suggested use
Figure 2 addresses the primary research question. Figure 1 provides descriptive
context. Figure 3 presents a secondary severity analysis. Figure S1 is intended
for supplementary material. If space is limited, prioritize Figure 2 and report
the two descriptive prevalences in the text; consider Figure 3 as a secondary figure.

Figure 1. Survey-weighted prevalence of frequent loneliness by self-reported
swallowing problems among adults aged 65 years or older, NHIS 2025.
Points indicate weighted prevalences and horizontal lines indicate design-based
95% confidence intervals calculated using a logit transformation. Frequent
loneliness was defined as always or usually feeling lonely (LONELY_A=1/2).
Swallowing problems refer to problems or difficulties during the preceding
12 months (VSLSWYR_A=1 versus 2). The analysis includes 8,231 adults with valid
exposure and outcome responses. When numerical age was unavailable, AGE65 was
used to confirm age 65 years or older for descriptive analyses. The group without
swallowing problems includes 7,545 adults and 355 events; the group with problems
includes 686 adults and 83 events. Sample sizes and event counts are unweighted.
Prevalences and confidence intervals incorporate the final annual adult weight
(WTFA_A), public-use strata (PSTRAT) and primary sampling units (PPSU). No
covariate adjustment was performed. These denominators differ from those in
the regression analyses. Events denote respondents reporting frequent loneliness,
not incident cases.

Figure 2. Prevalence ratios for frequent loneliness in adults with versus
without swallowing problems across sequential adjustment models, NHIS 2025.
All five models use the same complete-case sample of 8,094 adults aged 65 years
or older, with 429 frequent-loneliness events. Points represent prevalence ratios
(PRs) from survey-weighted Poisson models with a log link and design-based
variance estimation; lines represent 95% confidence intervals. The horizontal
axis is logarithmic and the dashed vertical line marks PR=1. M0 includes no
covariates. M1 adds age group (65-74, 75-84, 85+) and sex. M2 additionally adjusts
for educational attainment, family income-to-poverty ratio, partnership, living
alone, mobility limitations, hearing difficulties and history of stroke. M3
adds the frequency of in-person and telephone/video contact with close others
outside the household during a typical week. M3 is the primary model and is
highlighted by a diamond, color and row shading. M4 is an extended sensitivity
analysis that additionally adjusts for attendance at religious services and
meetings of clubs or organizations during the preceding year. Each contact or
participation variable is modeled as a four-category factor. Results from
income-adjusted models are pooled across all ten official income imputations
using Rubin's rules with finite complete-data degrees of freedom. For consistency
with the analysis script, models without income were also pooled across the ten
identical copies. Changes in estimates do not quantify causal effects or a
proportion mediated by social contact.

Figure 3. Secondary analysis of the severity of self-reported swallowing
difficulties and frequent loneliness, NHIS 2025.
Points and horizontal lines show PRs and 95% confidence intervals adjusted for
the M3 covariates, comparing a little difficulty, somewhere in between and a lot
of difficulty with no swallowing problems. These labels follow the questionnaire
response options. The original severity codes are ordered 1, 3 and 2; they do not
form an equally spaced continuous scale. The reference group has PR=1 and no
artificial confidence interval is plotted. The analysis includes 8,093 adults and
429 events. Group-specific sample sizes/event counts are 7,420/347 (no problems),
361/33 (a little), 199/27 (somewhere in between) and 113/22 (a lot). One member of
the common regression sample has no valid severity response. The small numbers
of events in the severity groups warrant caution. No trend test was performed;
points are not connected. Self-reported difficulty is not a clinically validated
dysphagia severity scale and the results do not establish a causal dose-response
relationship.

Supplementary Figure S1. Sensitivity analyses of the association between
swallowing problems and loneliness, NHIS 2025.
Points and lines indicate PRs and 95% confidence intervals. The primary M3 model
is included for comparison. Additional rows show M3 in all complete cases for
that model, M3 excluding proxy interviews, models without and with additional
adjustment for depressive feelings in an identical sample (N=8,055), and M3 with
a broader loneliness outcome. Depressive feelings were classified as daily/weekly
versus less often/never; this variable does not indicate a clinical depression
diagnosis. The broader outcome includes always, usually or sometimes feeling
lonely (2,120 events). Comparisons must account for differences in sample
composition and outcome definition. Supplementary analyses are not adjusted for
multiplicity. Some fitted values exceed 1 in the depressive-feelings-adjusted
and broader-outcome Poisson models; these fits should not be interpreted as
individual probabilities. Attenuation after adjustment for depressive feelings
does not establish causal mediation. The logistic sensitivity analysis is not
plotted on the same axis because its effect measure is an odds ratio, not a PR.

General interpretation
These are cross-sectional associations in the US civilian noninstitutionalized
population. Exposure and outcome are self-reported. The figures do not establish
causation and do not represent clinically confirmed dysphagia. "Events" means
respondents meeting the outcome definition, not incident events.

Files and reproduction
- Four PNG files: 3,000 x 1,920 pixels each, 300 dpi, white background.
- Four SVG files: scalable vector graphics. Cairo may store text as glyph paths;
  edit the R script to change wording reliably.
- NHIS2025_Figures_EN.pdf: four vector pages with embedded fonts.
- Data_*.tsv: plotted source data; estimates have not been refitted.
- 08_Figures_EN.R: complete generator using base R and grid; no new R packages.
- inputs/tables/: original tables needed by the generator.

Run from the original project directory:
Rscript -e "Sys.setlocale('LC_ALL','English_United States.utf8'); source('outputs/08_Figures_EN.R',encoding='UTF-8')"
To reproduce independently, place the generator at outputs/08_Figures_EN.R and
copy inputs/tables/ to outputs/tables/ within a new project directory. Run the
command from that project directory. Figures are written to outputs/figures_en/;
rerunning overwrites those figure files. No regression model is refitted.

Quality checks
The prevalence axis starts at zero. PR axes are logarithmic and mark PR=1.
No significance stars or truncated confidence intervals are used. All PDF pages
were rendered and checked for readability, clipping and marker placement.
Numbers, references and denominators were checked against the original result
tables. The English edition changes language and number formatting only.
