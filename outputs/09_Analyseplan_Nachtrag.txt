NHIS 2025 - Project 2: supplementary analysis plan
Dated 6 November 2026. Written after the primary results were known and before
the supplementary estimates below were calculated. These are post-primary,
supportive analyses, not externally preregistered confirmatory tests.

1. Standardized absolute prevalence
Fit survey-weighted logistic models with the same M3 covariates and common
complete-case sample. Use survey::svypredmeans (Korn/Graubard predictive margins)
to standardize each exposure level over the survey-weighted covariate distribution
of that analytic sample. Report prevalence for swallowing problems absent/present,
absolute prevalence difference, and marginal prevalence ratio. Within-imputation
variance includes model and estimated covariate-distribution components as
implemented in survey; use delta methods for logit prevalences and log PR.
Pool transformed estimates/variances over ten official income imputations with
Rubin's rules and finite complete-data residual design degrees of freedom.
Add M3 on the depression-complete sample with and without depressive-feelings
adjustment, plus the broader loneliness endpoint on the common sample. These are
model-standardized associations, not causal risks or potential-outcome effects.

2. Missingness and response weighting
Compare included (8,094) and excluded (398) older adults descriptively on observed
demographic and health characteristics, with missing values explicit. Response
weighting targets adults with known numerical age >=65 (8,468); the 24 adults
classified using AGE65 alone remain excluded from age-adjusted inference.
Fit a survey-logistic probability of inclusion in the common complete sample
using fully observed baseline characteristics: age group, region, adult household
count (1/2/3+), any children in household and proxy status. These predictors are
selected before the new fit and do not use outcome/exposure values missing from
the excluded cases. No p-value-driven selection. Stabilized response multipliers
are weighted complete-case proportion divided by fitted inclusion probability.
Fit M3 Poisson models under the resulting survey weights, over ten income
imputations. For untruncated multipliers, use stacked estimating-equation Taylor
linearization incorporating estimation of propensity coefficients, with score
contributions aggregated in the original full-sample strata/PSU design. Verify
the outcome-only component against survey::svyglm covariance. As an additional
diagnostic, winsorize multipliers at their unweighted 1st/99th percentiles among
included cases; report the result with conditional (fixed-weight) variance,
clearly distinguished from the primary response-weighting sensitivity.
Report propensity ranges, multiplier quantiles, combined-weight ranges and
weight-based effective sample size. This sensitivity assumes selection is
explainable by these measured baseline predictors and cannot resolve MNAR bias.

3. Descriptive Table 1 and precision
Display weighted categorical characteristics among adults >=65 with known
swallowing status (and separately the common complete sample). Use variable-
specific valid denominators, unweighted n and explicit missing counts. Income
percentages average the ten official imputations; report ranges of unweighted
category counts because imputed category membership varies. No baseline p-values.
For publication prevalences, implement NCHS proportion standards using design-
based variance, effective denominator n capped at nominal n, Korn-Graubard beta
confidence limits with finite-df adjustment, nominal/effective n >=30, absolute
CI width and relative-width rules, and review flags for 0/1 or low design df.
Evaluate complements separately. Apply these standards to descriptive proportions,
not directly to regression PR. Retain old primary logit intervals; the new table
identifies the additional Korn-Graubard intervals and precision assessment.

4. Full loneliness distribution and ordinal-model assessment
Report five survey-weighted LONELY_A categories by swallowing status using all
valid exposure/outcome responses. On the common complete-case sample, fit four
survey-logistic cumulative dichotomizations (>=rarely, >=sometimes, >=usually,
always) with all M3 covariates. Jointly estimate coefficient covariance across
thresholds using survey::svyglm influence functions and survey::svyrecvar.
Compare each of the three other threshold-specific slopes with the primary
>=usually slope for all 23 non-intercept coefficients, pooling contrasts over
ten imputations. A Bonferroni familywise 0.05 threshold across the 69 slope
contrasts is a conservative diagnostic of proportional-odds adequacy. Report
the exposure-specific threshold estimates and diagnostics. Fit a common
survey-proportional-odds model only if no adjusted diagnostic rejects and model
fits are stable. Otherwise retain threshold-specific results and document why a
single ordinal OR is not supported. Failure to reject is not proof of adequacy.
